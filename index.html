How to Use This Tool

      
    
        
  • Step 1 — Paste or Load a Sample: Paste any article, essay, email, or creative story into the Input Passage box. When pasting directly from Gemini or ChatGPT web pages, the tool intercepts rich-text formatting to preserve `**bold**` and `*italic*` emphasis for Tier 6 analysis.
    • 
        
  • Step 2 — Choose Your Genre Baseline: Select Web / General Prose, Creative / Narrative Fiction, Academic / Preprint, Email / Memo, or Historical / 18th-C Oratory. Baselines adjust expected rates for nominalizations, em-dashes, and triads.
    • 
        
  • Step 3 — Inspect Evidence Spans: Switch the input toggle from Editor to Evidence Spans to see every matched tell color-coded in context. Hover over any highlighted span to inspect its `rule_id`, model era, and academic source.
    • 
      


      
Understanding the Score Bands (0–100)

      
    
        
  • 0–29: LOW (Human-Consistent) Isolated stylistic patterns or historical rhetoric without multi-tier LLM corroboration, or strong negative-weight human signs.
    • 
        
  • 30–59: MIXED / PROBABLE AI Moderate clusters of phrase frames, lexical markers, or Biber syntactic features. Common in lightly edited AI drafts or heavily templated corporate prose.
    • 
        
  • 60–89: HIGH DENSITY Multi-tier saturation across measured vocabulary, rhetorical negation frames, participial clauses, and structural formatting.
    • 
        
  • 90–100: ARTIFACT FLOOR Triggered automatically when Tier 1 deterministic residue (`contentReference[oaicite:0]`, `utm_source=chatgpt.com`, unfilled `[Company Name]` placeholders, `↩` footnote arrows, or chat openers like `"Certainly! Here is..."`) is present in raw input, or when multi-tier points + co-occurrence reach 90+.
    • 
      

    


    
    

      
The Five Design Rules

      
    
        
  1. Tier the signals: Deterministic chat residue (Tier 1) sets a score floor of 90. Probabilistic signals (Tiers 2–6) are weighted and capped so no single habit dominates.
    2. 
        
  2. Normalize per 1,000 words: Raw counts penalize long human documents. All probabilistic tiers scale against word count (excluding fenced code blocks and URLs).
    3. 
        
  3. Version by model era: Consecutive model versions share only 18.7%–45.5% of their tells (Graphite 2026). Every rule carries an era fingerprint.
    4. 
        
  4. Never invert a reversed signal silently: Em-dashes (`0.014/1k` in Gemini 3.1 Pro, `0.015/1k` in Opus 5.5 vs. `3.14/1k` human) and the classic 8-word delve/tapestry list now run below the human rate in 2026 models. High rates flag 2023–25 models; low rates are never scored as AI.
    5. 
        
  5. Show evidence, not a verdict: Every matched span is highlighted with its source and rule ID, and Human-Leaning features deduct up to `-25 pts`.
    6.

The Model Drift Problem & Non-Native English Guardrails

      
Lexical Drift (2023 → 2026): Model families diverge rapidly. Graphite's Oct 2026 corpus shows that the classic 8-word list (delve, tapestry, nuance, crucial, landscape, foster, stakeholder, realm) runs at 2.62/1k in 2024–25 models (GPT-4.1, Opus 4, Gemini 2.5 Pro) but dropped to 0.31/1k in 2026 frontier models—below the human baseline of 0.44/1k. Conversely, 2026 models exhibit sharp spikes in corrective/importance framing ("the _ is not simply" at 576x in GPT-6 Astra; "this matters" at 116x in Claude Opus 5.5) and Biber syntactic features (participial clauses and paired adjectives).

      
False-Positive Risk on Non-Native English (Liang et al., 2023): Commercial perplexity detectors flagged 61.3% of genuine TOEFL essays as AI-written because non-native speakers use a more constrained, formal transition vocabulary. This tool never uses standalone perplexity and requires multi-tier co-occurrence across syntax, frames, and lexicon.
